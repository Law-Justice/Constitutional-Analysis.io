The Constitution: Constitutional Analysis

You will show what you understand about the structure of the United States Constitution and how it has changed. There are three ways to do it.

Choose ONE option below. You do not need to do more than one.

Every option asks for the same thinking:

  • A summary of each of the seven Articles. For each one, address: which branch (or branches) of government it covers, the primary powers it grants, its primary limitations, and any other information it gives about the structure of government.
  • A short analysis of each of the 27 Amendments. For each one, address: what freedoms it protects, provides, or takes away, and what era of American history it was written in and what context that gives the amendment.
  • In the games, a final reflection that looks at the whole Constitution through a legal lens.

Want to read the whole Constitution first? Read it on Senate.gov. Every option uses the same document.

Pick your option

Option 1: 3D game (pick a look)

Walk Philadelphia in 1787 and listen to the delegates argue out the Constitution. Climb parkour trials to the scrolls and summarize each Article, then enter the Hall of Echoes, where eight guides walk you through the amendments from 1791 to 1992 with a lawyer’s eye. It is the same story and the same assignment in both looks, so pick the one you like.

Choose this if you like games and have a laptop or computer. It is the most demanding option for your device.

Block-world (third person) Storybook adventure (camera behind you)

You submit: your completed essay, a Word document the game gives you at the end.

Option 2: Pixel-art game

Walk a top-down, pixel-art Philadelphia. Hear the debates, summarize each Article at the scrolls, and then follow the amendments through the Hall of Echoes. It runs on almost any device, including phones.

Choose this if you like games but have a slower device, or you want something without jumping.

Play the pixel-art game

You submit: your completed essay, a Word document the game gives you at the end.

Option 3: Written assignment

Read the Constitution and write your summaries and analyses straight into the Word document. No game needed.

Choose this if you would rather read and write in a document, or you can’t use a game on your device.

Download the written assignment

You submit: your completed document. It opens in Word or Google Docs.

How to turn it in

  1. Choose one option and finish it.
  2. If you played a game: after the final reflection at the end, press Esc (or tap the Satchel button) and open the Case file tab. Choose Download completed essay (Word document). That document is your essay, with all of your writing in it, in the same order as the written assignment.
  3. If you chose the written assignment: complete the document (open it in Google Docs or Word) and save it.
  4. Attach your file to the assignment in Google Classroom and turn it in.

Before you start

  • Stay on one device and one browser. A game saves your progress in the browser you are using. If you switch devices or clear your browsing data, you will start over.
  • Wait until you are finished to download. The essay document unlocks only after you complete the final reflection.
  • Games are not a race. The game asks for a summary of all seven Articles and an analysis of all 27 Amendments, just like the written assignment. Plan several class periods.
  • Platforming too hard in the 3D game? Press Esc, open the Play tab, and turn on Platforming assist. Or switch to Option 2.
  • No mouse needed. In every game you can read, choose, write and use your satchel with the keyboard alone. Use the arrow keys (or W A S D) to move between choices, E or Enter to select, number keys to jump to an answer, Ctrl+Enter to save what you are writing, and Esc to open your satchel. Hints appear at the bottom of each dialogue box.
  • Wrong choice? You can switch. Each game has a link back to this page on its first screen.

Nothing you type is sent to a server. Your work stays on your device until you attach it in Google Classroom.
